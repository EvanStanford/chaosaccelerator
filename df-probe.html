Double-float (df) precision probe

Double-float arithmetic depends on compensation steps ((a+b)-a-style round-off recovery) that are algebraically redundant and only work because IEEE rounding makes them not redundant. GLSL ES 3.00 has no precise qualifier, so a shader compiler that reassociates float math is free to fold them to nothing - silently. This page measures whether that happens on this machine/driver, both for raw arithmetic and for physics-df.js's veiled version of it.

4. What that actually buys, zoom by zoom

The same scene - a ball dropped between two angled deflectors in a wrapped frame, 500 steps, colored by where it ends up - rendered by both shaders as the view zooms in.

The number that matters is the correlation against the float64 JS engine, which is the answer both shaders are approximating. An earlier version of this page instead measured how many neighbouring pixel pairs came out byte-identical, and that flattered the df pass by about three decades: a pair differing by pure quantization noise counts as "resolved" under that metric, so it credits detail that isn't there. Correlation against the truth can't be fooled that way - noise decorrelates. It's sampled along a row of 24 points, each re-run in float64 on the CPU.
